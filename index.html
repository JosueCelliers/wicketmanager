The Player Career

Read the ball.
Then play it.

The delivery reveal · new in 1.7

See it coming.

Every delivery is read before you face it — loose, testing or dangerous, with the ball in words: "full and swinging in late", "the wrong 'un, at the stumps". Then you set the dial and play. A wicket is never something that just happened to you.

  • About one ball in eight is hidden — "he hides it, no read" — and an elite wrist-spinner hides more than a club medium-pacer. Reading him is a skill, and there is a skill lane for it
  • The cricket underneath is exactly as honest as before: ignore the read and you bat the same game. Only playing the ball you were shown changes anything
  • Bat blind — reads off — for one and a half times the XP. A hard mode you choose
  • Run your own way — singles, normal or run hard — and the dial prices the risk before the ball is bowled
  • Hold the ball button to keep the balls coming; it slows down by itself for the big moments
Attack the loose ones · see off the dangerous
The ladder

School. Domestic.
Franchise. Country.

Start at sixteen in school cricket, or at seventeen on a domestic contract. Your place in the side is a sum you can see — rating, form, recent runs, promise — and the man ahead of you is named, with the gap.

  • A big season opens the door young: score like an eighty-five and the selectors look at you whatever your rating says. Stop performing and the level sends you back
  • Three formats in every season — T20, fifty-over, and the four-day round of first-class cricket at domestic level. Tests come with the country
  • Choose the formats you play: retire from one for a record that says one thing, and one return in a career
  • Three franchises bid for you. Your country picks you. Nobody else does
Not selected is a moment, not a line
Skills, and the armband

Your tree.
Your call.

Four lanes a side, every node a thing that happens at the crease rather than a number on a card — and the fourth lane grants information: what the bowler is trying to do, the field they have set, the one thing driving your out-chance right now.

  • Getting in, matchups, tempo, the read — with the bat. The new ball, control, the close, the read — with the ball
  • Captain? Pick who bowls the next over, set the field, tell the attack how hard to hunt. An over at a time when you are not bowling
  • A re-cut every winter: take a node back and spend its points somewhere else
  • Ceilings that hold: a career is a story about a player, not a shop
Information cannot be bought. It can be learned
The record

Every innings
counts.

A rating for every innings and every spell, a report that says why the number moved, a record book split the way cricket splits it — league, cup, first-class, international — and a son who can pick up where you left off.

  • The six-ball trial before you even have a name, then a career that remembers all of it
  • Milestones, duels with the bowlers who have your number, momentum you can feel
  • The weekly challenge: five seasons, one measured par, a result card you can share
  • Retire at 34 or play until the body decides at 43 — then the Hall of Fame, and your son

The match centre

Play every ball.

No dice roll. A broadcast-style scorebug, a live lower-third, and a decision on every delivery.

  • Read the pitch and the forecast, then win or lose the toss for real
  • Pick your bowler every over, set the field, react to the conditions, give the team talk at the break
  • Three XIs from one squad: a team sheet reads each man's record in the format he is being picked for
  • Rain interruptions with proper DLS targets, and chase pressure that makes weak batters wobble
Highlights mode for when you're short on time
The auction

Going once…
going twice…

Every franchise season opens with a live auction. Retain your core, then outbid seven rival owners for the best of forty lots with a fixed purse.

  • Rivals bid to real valuations and real squad needs, and they bid back
  • Power bids to shake out anyone who isn't serious
  • Marquee lots, bidding wars, and a summary that tells you who overpaid
Don't blink first
Three jobs

Club. Franchise.
Country.

Make your manager, start at a club, and earn the bigger jobs. Offers follow your reputation, and the phone rings when you've earned it.

  • Hold three jobs at once, each in its own window of the year
  • Your club players get called up by their country: one player, one career, everywhere they play
  • A T20 World Championship every two years, a 50-over World Cup every four, Test series on the summer calendar, world rankings that decide who qualifies
  • Get sacked from one job and the rest of your career carries on. Never a dead end
Retire at 70 with a legacy, or keep going
The board

Lift the title.
Or get sacked trying.

Boards set objectives, sponsors set conditions, and confidence rises and falls with every result. Every board in the game will sack you eventually. Outlast them.

  • Transfers, contracts, form and morale, injuries, a youth academy where nobodies become legends
  • Sponsor deals with real trade-offs, gate receipts, merchandise, a finances card that tells you why
  • 130+ achievements, career records, a Hall of Fame, season history you can look back on

Names you can edit.
Records you can't fake.

Forty-two countries, fifty-six franchises, a hundred and twenty clubs, and 3,488 players who each arrive with a career that already happened — and keep playing it whether you are watching or not.

  • Every player's record in every format, world rankings, country records: played, won, lost, drawn, tied

How the player career works

Six balls,
then a name.

Face six balls

Before there is a form, there is a trial: six balls with the bat and six with the ball. Then pick an archetype — the accumulator, the destroyer, the seamer, the spinner — and its ceilings are yours.

Fight for your place

Your standing is a sum you can see, every matchday. Runs and wickets move it. When you are left out, the game tells you who went in ahead of you and by how much.

Climb, or be sent back

A monster season earns the next level whatever your rating says; a bench-warming one costs it. Franchise money, a country's cap, the armband — and a son to carry the name when you stop.

How the manager career works

Earn your positions.

Make your manager

Name, nationality, age and a background with a real perk: ex-player, coach, analyst or businessman. Your own nation's side and leagues call more often.

Collect jobs, don't just switch them

Start at a club. A T20 franchise and a national side arrive as offers when your reputation earns them, and you can hold all three.

Play until they retire you

Auctions every year, bilateral series, Test summers, world events, call-ups, sackings. Fifty years, then a legacy ceremony, then keep playing if you want.
